# Video notes, Member 4 (about 3 min): animation and dissipation

- (Show `animation_default.mp4`.) This is the default system: 12 particles, dt = 0.01, 2000 velocity Verlet steps. Grey lines are pairs closer than rc = 2; time, E_total and the pair count are at the top.
- We used the movie as a diagnostic and checked each thing with numbers (show the table in section 2 of the report). Nothing leaves the box (coordinates stay in 0 to 10), no particle stays on a wall for more than 1 step, and the largest step is 1.025 times |v| dt, so there are no jumps.
- What the movie does show is strong clustering (show `diagnostics_default.png`): pairs go from 8 to 40, at one moment all 66 pairs are inside rc, and K goes from 3.3 to 280.
- Why: the pair energy at rc is 10.8, not 0. In shifted form each pair is a well 10.8 deep, while the barrier against overlapping is only 4.8, so particles fall in, overlap and heat up. Two particles alone just pass through each other; it takes a third particle to make a cluster.
- (Show `animation_dt0.1.mp4` and `diagnostics_dt.png`.) With dt = 0.1 the movie still looks like a gas, but the energy rises by up to 680 within t = 20 and a single step can be longer than rc. The movie alone would not have told us this.
- Dissipation: we added F = -gamma v. Our step is velocity Verlet with the drag implicit in the second half-kick, which is just one division because the drag is linear. It is second order (show `stepper_check.csv`: error ratio 4 when dt is halved).
- (Show `compare_gamma0_vs_gamma0.5.mp4`, then `compare_conservative_dissipative.png`.) Left, gamma = 0 keeps moving forever; right, gamma = 0.5 looks still by about t = 8 (K = 1.02), and passes our steady-state test at t = 30.9.
- Energy balance (show `energy_balance.png` and its table): the drop of the shifted energy equals the drag work to within 1% for gamma >= 0.1 and 3.8% for gamma = 0.05. Most of the mismatch comes from steps where a pair crosses the cutoff.
- Steady-state criterion: mean speed below 1e-3 AND energy change over 10 time units below 1e-3 of E(0), holding until the end of the run. The energy test alone accepts states that are still visibly moving.
- (Show the relaxation table and `relaxation_time_vs_gamma.png`.) Up to gamma = 2, t_relax goes like 1/gamma (349, 192, 31, 16, 8.3) and K decays exactly as exp(-gamma t). Above about gamma = 4 the system is over-damped and relaxation gets slower again (6.3, 13.1, 16.7). The fastest relaxation is near critical damping.
- (Show `final_states.png`.) gamma also changes the final state. Small gamma heats up first and ends as one 11-particle cluster with much lower energy (E_sh = -563); large gamma freezes the starting 7-particle group in place (E_sh about -220). It is like annealing vs quenching.
- One issue we found: the handout wall clamp adds energy in long runs (+430 of +684 over t = 600). A mirror reflection reduces the total to +46.
